ORDER THRESHOLDS FOR STRICT MSA COUPLING JETS — 2026-10-07

Scope and outcome
-----------------
The question must be split by anomaly, tail class, thermodynamic route, and
quantifier. There is no single earliest order common to the phenomena below.
Here a_[N] = d(beta p_[N])/d rho is the strict compressibility-route Taylor
jet, not the square of a truncated Baxter factor. A denotes its Baxter
amplitude and must not be confused with a or the Helmholtz free energy.
The reference is scalar PY hard spheres, sigma=1, eta=pi*rho/6, with the
coupling tau multiplying prescribed attractive Yukawa amplitudes.

Results supported by proofs or interval certificates:
* An amplitude-equation fold/no-real-root gap does not arise from evaluating
  any finite polynomial jet at finite real tau, provided its seed Jacobian
  and coefficients are regular. This is evaluability, not accuracy.
* Pure attractive single Yukawa: fixed-density sign reentry on cooling first
  becomes possible at order 2. In fact it occurs at order 2 for EVERY z>0
  and 0<eta<1. First order is strictly decreasing in tau and cannot reenter.
* Pure attractive single Yukawa: first order has at most one pressure loop,
  for EVERY z>0 and T>0. A second-order four-spinodal counterexample is
  enclosed by interval arithmetic. Thus the minimum order admitting extra
  pressure loops in this class is exactly 2.
* Long attraction / short repulsion, with net integrated attraction: a
  negative finite-k spectrum at mechanically positive k=0 is possible at
  order 1. Zeroth-order modulus-square spectra cannot be negative.

Open distinctions:
* Extra supporting coexistence pairs: first order is excluded for pure
  single-Yukawa attraction; order 3 has independently integrated numerical
  examples. Whether order 2 admits them remains unresolved. The order-3
  example is not an interval proof of the coexistence root/support condition.
* Earliest finite-k failure for pure single-Yukawa attraction is unresolved;
  a third-order supporting-endpoint numerical counterexample is available in
  ../low_order_normal_tail. The two-tail order-1 certificate is not an endpoint.
* No proof that EVERY N>1 admits extra loops for some z,T, or that all
  higher pressure coefficients are positive. No universal safe order for
  arbitrary multirate tails or mixtures has been established.
* Extra loops/extra tangents are changes in approximate phase topology;
  they are not, by themselves, proofs of physically nonexistent phases.
  A negative inverse structure factor cannot describe a stable homogeneous
  fluctuation spectrum, but does not identify the actual nonuniform phase.

Exact first-two-coefficient derivation
-------------------------------------
Put w=1-eta, E=exp(-z), and define
  J=z-2+(z+2)E,
  K=2-(z^2+2z+2)E,
  H0=z^2-2+2(z+1)E=(z+2)J+K,
  P=z^3 w^2+6 eta w H0+18 eta^2 J,
  L=w z+1+2 eta,
  H=w H0+6 eta J.
For a_[2]=c0+c1*tau+c2*tau^2 the coefficients are
  c0=((1+2 eta)/w^2)^2,
  c1=-24 eta z^4 (1+2 eta)L/P^2,
  c2=144 eta^2 z^11 w^7 H/P^5.

msa_order_threshold_symbolic.py derives these formulas directly from the
scalar PY/BH amplitude residual, checks both amplitude equations through
order 2, and checks the coefficient and discriminant identities exactly.
This is not an imported energy-route inverse-temperature expansion.
Independent long-double series-engine comparisons cover 35 points with
eta=.01,.1,.3,.5,.7 and z=.2,1,1.8,5,12,30,100. The maximum scaled
coefficient discrepancy is 7.83e-12, including cancellation at small z.
Those floating comparisons are a cross-check, not the proof of the formulas.

J(0)=K(0)=0; J'=1-(z+1)e^-z>0 and K'=z^2 e^-z>0 for z>0.
Therefore J,K,H0,P,H,L are positive, c0>0, c1<0, c2>0.
These sign claims for the explicit formulas are proved in Lean.

The discriminant is positive, not merely nonnegative:
  Delta=c1^2-4c0c2 = 576 eta^2 z^8(1+2 eta)^2 V/P^5,
  V=L^2 P-z^3 w^3 H=sum_{i=0}^4 V_i eta^i w^(4-i),
where each V_i is strictly positive:
  V0=z^3[1+2(z+1)(1-E)],
  V1=6[(3+5z+2z^2)K+(z^3+7z^2+9z+4)J],
  V2=9[(6+5z)H0+(2z+2z^2)J],
  V3=54[H0+2(z+1)J],
  V4=162J.
Both the decomposition identity and its positivity are Lean proofs.

Consequently the two positive roots
  tau_minus=(-c1-sqrt(Delta))/(2c2),
  tau_plus =(-c1+sqrt(Delta))/(2c2)
bound a negative interval. In particular,
  a_[2](-c1/(2c2))=-Delta/(4c2)<0,
  a_[2](-c1/c2)=c0>0.
The constructive Lean theorem establishes the ordered positive couplings
without relying on numerical root location. First order c0+c1*tau cannot
return positive after becoming negative. This proves the exact threshold 2
for fixed-density sign reentry, but does not imply a global coexistence pair.
For unit amplitude, the negative-temperature window is
1/tau_plus < T < 1/tau_minus; below 1/tau_plus, order 2 is positive again.
Strong-coupling reentry is not claimed to lie within the coupling radius.

Why first order has at most one loop (single attractive Yukawa only)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Define its spinodal temperature T1=-c1/c0 and t=eta/(1-eta)>0. With
  a=z+1, b=z^3, c=6H0, d=18J,
  S=(1+t)(1+3t), R=b+c*t+d*t^2,
we obtain
  T1(t,z)=24 z^4 t(a+3t)/(S R^2),
  dT1/dt=24 z^4 Q(t)/(S^2 R^3).
SymPy checks this transformation and derivative exactly. Q=sum q_i t^i:
  q0=b*a>0,
  q1=6b-c*a (either sign),
  q2=-3b*a+12b-8c*a-3d*a,
  q3=-9c*a-12c-16d*a-6d<0,
  q4=-3(6c+5d*a+12d)<0,
  q5=-36d<0.

The nontrivial q2 sign is also universal. For
  G(z)=(z+1)H0-(2/3)z^3,
G(0)=0 and G'=H0+2z[1-(z+1)e^-z]>0, so
  (z+1)H0>(2/3)z^3,
  q2=3z^3(3-z)-48(z+1)H0-54(z+1)J<0.
Lean proves this stronger kernel bound and q2<0.

Now Q(t)/t=q0/t+q1+q2*t+q3*t^2+q4*t^3+q5*t^4 is strictly
decreasing on t>0. It tends to +infinity at 0 and -infinity at infinity,
so it has exactly one zero. Lean proves strict decrease and at most one
positive derivative-numerator root for these explicit coefficients.
The endpoint limits, root existence by continuity, and thermodynamic
interpretation in this paragraph are ordinary mathematical arguments,
not additional fully formalized Lean theorems.

T1 tends to zero at both endpoints and has one positive maximum Tc1.
Since a_[1]=c0(1-T1/T), for 0<T<Tc1 the set a_[1]<0 is one connected
density interval; for T>=Tc1 it is empty, apart from a critical touching
zero at equality. Thus first order cannot have an extra pressure loop.

It also cannot have two distinct gas/liquid common-tangent constructions.
At equal pressure, the chemical-potential difference has derivative
  d(mu_liquid-mu_gas)/dp=1/rho_liquid-1/rho_gas<0.
There are only two increasing pressure branches separated by the one loop,
so this difference has at most one zero. This establishes uniqueness, not
an all-k homogeneous-stability or accuracy guarantee. A possible fluid
freezing transition is outside this homogeneous calculation.

Certified second-order extra-loop example
----------------------------------------
Pure attraction: z=5, T=0.06259942900035, unit amplitude. Directed interval
evaluation of the explicit formulas above, at both 30 and 60 decimal digits,
gives these signs (rounded values here, actual enclosures in JSON):
  rho=.01 : a_[2]=+0.5892788604,
  rho=.10 : a_[2]=-0.9865538281,
  rho=.27 : a_[2]=+0.1404807565,
  rho=.50 : a_[2]=-9.0727022804,
  rho=1.3 : a_[2]=+212.8944087.
P>0 and eta<1 throughout this interval, hence a_[2] is continuous.
The intermediate-value theorem gives at least one distinct spinodal root
in each of the four disjoint intervals between these points. Two separated
negative-slope intervals therefore occur already at order 2. This certifies
extra-loop existence without relying on a density spline or a rounded
double root. It does NOT prove exactly four roots or two supporting tangents.
The positive island is within the envelope of the outer spinodal roots;
it should not be called part of the actual unstable set {a_[2]<0}.

Certified first-order finite-k counterexample with normal tail ordering
----------------------------------------------------------------------
rho=.3, z=(1.8,12), K=(1,-6), T=.2. Attraction is longer ranged,
repulsion shorter ranged. The repulsive contact amplitude is strong;
this is not a theorem for weak repulsion. The integrated attraction is
  (1+1.8)/1.8^2 - 6*(1+12)/12^2 = .322530864... > .322.
Thus it satisfies net integrated attraction despite short contact repulsion.
Explicit first-order PY/BH amplitudes, evaluated by directed interval
arithmetic at 30 and 60 decimal digits, certify
  D_[1](0)>2.805,
  D_[1](5)<-.202.
The independent series/spectrum engine agrees to ordinary floating accuracy.
It follows that first order cannot universally guarantee a nonnegative
finite-k spectrum under these tail assumptions. This is a state example;
no Maxwell endpoint or globally supporting tangent is asserted for it.

Certification boundaries and reproduction
----------------------------------------
Run from the repository root:
  python Lean_code/scripts/msa_order_threshold_symbolic.py
  python Lean_code/scripts/msa_order_threshold_interval_check.py
Run from Lean_code:
  lake build LeanCode

The interval calculation uses mpmath.iv with exact decimal/integer inputs,
an enclosed pi, real/complex elementary interval exp, and interval arithmetic
on explicit coefficients. There is no interval LU solve or root optimization.
It assumes the correctness of mpmath's directed interval elementary library;
it is not a Lean kernel certificate for pi/exp numerical enclosures. Raising
precision is an implementation cross-check, not what makes the sign proof.

FiniteOrderThresholds.lean contains no sorry or new physical axiom. The
project guards the second-order reentry, first-order derivative-root uniqueness,
and small-coupling positivity theorems against axiom-footprint changes.
Only [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound] occur. Full LeanCode build passes
(8816 jobs). The identification of the displayed formulas with physical
OZ/MSA derivatives is checked by SymPy, NOT fully bridged inside Lean.
Pressure integrations, the full first-order loop/coexistence topology proof,
and the transcendental interval enclosures remain outside Lean's kernel.

At any fixed regular state and wavevector with positive HS reference value,
continuity makes every finite-order polynomial positive at sufficiently small
coupling. Lean proves this pointwise fact. It is not uniform in density,
wavevector, order, or z, and does not refute the different assertion
"for every N>1 there exists some bad z,T". That assertion is still open here.

Literature search and paper wording
----------------------------------
The targeted search found convergence/singular-solution analyses, not a theorem
giving a common minimum coupling order for all these failures. A negative
search result is not proof that no such theorem exists. See literature.txt.

The paper can now distinguish rigorously: evaluability at all finite orders;
one-loop topology at first order for pure single-Yukawa attraction; universal
fixed-density second-order reentry in that same class; extra pressure loops
possible from second order; extra supporting tangents as a separate test;
finite-k positivity not guaranteed even at first order for normal competing
tails. It should not call first order universally physically correct, nor
call extra phases strictly nonphysical without a benchmark.
The FMSA_paper thread has been notified. Its rewritten main/SI sections were
reviewed read-only and preserve these distinctions; no paper TeX was edited.
