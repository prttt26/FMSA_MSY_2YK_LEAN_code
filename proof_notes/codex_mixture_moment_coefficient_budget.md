# Fixed reference moment for mixture frequency grids

`MixtureMomentCoefficientBudget.lean` removes the amplitude-dependent first-moment bound from the finite-frequency-grid noescape theorem. Fix four uniform absolute coefficient caps `Abar`, `Qbar`, `Wbar`, and `Cbar` for the actual BH families `A`, `q′`, `W`, and `C`. Define a reference Baxter kernel with `A=Abar`, `q′=-Qbar`, `W=Wbar`, and `C=Cbar`, at the same rates and diameters. The minus sign makes its compact linear contribution nonnegative because `r-edgeHi≤0` in the core.

`actual_q_abs_le_reference_q` proves pointwise `|q_actual,ij(r)| ≤ q_reference,ij(r)` for all real `r`, including negative support edges, the compact core, exterior tails, and empty tail sets. It uses only the four coefficient caps and elementary absolute-value inequalities. `referenceMoment_integrable` proves the fixed integral `∫ |r| |q_reference,ij(r)| dr` exists for positive rates and nonnegative diameters. Consequently `actual_first_moment_le_referenceMoment` and its path version bound every actual first absolute moment by that **same fixed integral**.

`hs_coefficient_grid_uniform_amplitude_bound` combines this reference moment with the previous finite-frequency-grid theorem. Its certificate inputs are now the four uniform coefficient caps; nonzero determinants and inverse norm bounds at finitely many frequency samples for every coupling on the prefix; and finite-window coverage using `momentBudget(ρ, referenceMoment)`. It concludes one bound on the original `(D,G)` amplitudes. No independent moment bound or all-frequency continuum nonvanishing premise remains.

The reference integral depends only on rates, diameters and the chosen coefficient caps. A concrete parameter certificate still needs rigorous upper evaluation of that fixed integral, sample determinant/inverse bounds uniform in coupling, and coverage of the explicit frequency window. The theorem does not assert that a float scan supplies those proofs.

The next theorem removes even that fixed integral from the **coverage expression**. Choose `δ > 0` below every tail rate. `referenceQ_nonneg` proves positivity of the reference kernel when the caps are nonnegative. On its support `r ≥ edgeLo`, the inequality `|r| ≤ (|edgeLo| + 1/δ) exp(δ(r-edgeLo))` holds. `referenceMoment_le_transformMomentUpper` integrates this inequality and identifies the exponential-weighted reference integral with the actual `Qhat` transform at `s=-δ`, where every tail denominator is safely inside its convergence domain. Thus `transformMomentUpper` is a finite real rational-exponential formula in the supplied data and coefficient caps, with no integral left to evaluate. `hs_transform_grid_uniform_amplitude_bound` uses that expression directly in the finite-grid noescape certificate. Uniform sample determinant and inverse bounds across the coupling prefix remain the concrete interval obligations.

`MixtureSampleInverseCertificate.lean` reduces the inverse bound to finite polynomial data at each sample: if `m>0`, `m≤‖det F(a)‖`, and `complexNormSum(adj F(a))≤B`, then `complexNormSum(F(a)⁻¹)≤B/m`. Its physical-factor slab theorem feeds that bound into the actual Fourier perturbation estimate. For a concrete path, the determinant margins and adjugate bounds must hold uniformly in coupling; the adjugate is a finite polynomial in sample matrix entries.
