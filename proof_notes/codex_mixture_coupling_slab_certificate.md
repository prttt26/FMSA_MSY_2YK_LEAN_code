# Finite coupling boxes for mixture amplitude noescape

`MixtureCouplingSlabCertificate.lean` completes the second finite-covering step of the Fourier route. The frequency direction already has the arithmetic mesh in `MixtureFrequencyGridCoverage`. Here a finite family of centers covers the coupling prefix through coefficient-difference boxes for the actual BH families `A`, `q′`, `W`, and `C`.

At fixed positive rates and diameters, the actual full-line Baxter kernel is linear in those four coefficient families. Lean proves the exact difference identity and bounds its absolute value by a fixed reference kernel made from the box widths `Abar`, `-Qbar`, `Wbar`, `Cbar`. Fourier integration then bounds every entry change uniformly in real frequency. The density-weighted matrix change is bounded by `referenceMatrixMassBudget`; Lean bounds that integral by `transformMatrixMassBudget`, a finite expression using the actual real transform `Qhat(-δ)` with `0 < δ < z_u` for every tail. No numerical integration premise occurs in the public transform-box theorem.

For a center sample with nonzero factor determinant and inverse entry-norm sum at most `Cbase`, the inequality `Cbase * transformMatrixMassBudget ≤ 1/2` proves that all factors in its coefficient box remain invertible and have inverse entry-norm sum at most `2 N Cbase`. `path_samples_of_finite_coefficient_boxes` applies this argument to a finite center family and finite sample set. The end-to-end `hs_finite_center_mesh_amplitude_bound` also derives the four global BH caps from finite center caps plus box widths. It combines these with the frequency mesh, explicit high-frequency cutoff and actual same-HS-seed MSA root path, obtaining one bound on the original `(D,G)` amplitudes throughout `[0,b)`.

The public `hs_finite_center_mesh_adjugate_amplitude_bound` uses determinant margins `m(c,a)>0` and adjugate entry-norm bounds `Badj(c,a)` at the finite center samples. It obtains the center inverse cap `Badj/m` from `MixtureSampleInverseCertificate.inverse_normSum_le_adjugate_over_margin` inside Lean. Thus the sample hypotheses can be validated by interval enclosures of determinant and adjugate entries, with no inverse expression in the certificate statement.

This is a conditional certificate interface. For a concrete mixture, rigorous interval work must still establish: (1) an actual root path from the HS seed with the stated continuity; (2) that every path value lies in one of finitely many coefficient boxes; (3) finite center coefficient caps and center-sample determinant and inverse bounds, or determinant/adjugate bounds via `MixtureSampleInverseCertificate`; and (4) the displayed arithmetic inequalities. In particular, a float scan of path points does not prove box coverage. The theorem does not establish a universal Taylor radius or identify a first singularity.

Validation: the guarded Lean checker audits each theorem's exact axiom footprint. All fourteen theorems use only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`; the foundation package remains frozen at 1.0.17.
